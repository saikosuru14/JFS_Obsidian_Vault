# Data Structures & Algorithms

> Domain hub — the data structures, algorithms, and problem-solving patterns for coding interviews and everyday engineering.

## Overview
DSA is the toolkit for representing data efficiently and manipulating it correctly and fast. This domain builds from **complexity analysis** (how to reason about cost) through the core data structures (arrays, lists, stacks/queues, hashing, trees, graphs) and the major algorithmic paradigms (sorting/searching, recursion/backtracking, dynamic programming, greedy), ending with the **problem-solving patterns** that recur in interviews.

## Modules (Learning Order)
1. **[[Complexity Analysis Index|Complexity Analysis]]** — Big O, time/space, amortized.
2. **[[Arrays and Strings Index|Arrays & Strings]]** — two pointers, sliding window, prefix sum.
3. **[[Linked Lists Index|Linked Lists]]** — singly/doubly, fast & slow pointers, cycle detection.
4. **[[Stacks and Queues Index|Stacks & Queues]]** — stack, queue, deque, monotonic stack.
5. **[[Hashing Index|Hashing]]** — hash tables, collisions, hashing patterns.
6. **[[Trees Index|Trees]]** — binary tree, BST, traversals, heap, trie.
7. **[[Graphs Index|Graphs]]** — BFS/DFS, shortest path, topological sort, union-find.
8. **[[Sorting and Searching Index|Sorting & Searching]]** — sorts, binary search.
9. **[[Recursion and Backtracking Index|Recursion & Backtracking]]** — recursion, backtracking, divide & conquer.
10. **[[Dynamic Programming Index|Dynamic Programming]]** — memoization, tabulation, patterns.
11. **[[Greedy and Patterns Index|Greedy & Patterns]]** — greedy, coding patterns, interview strategy.

## How This Connects
- Built on **[[Computer Science]]** fundamentals; implemented with **[[Collections Framework|Java Collections]]** (`ArrayList`, `HashMap`, `PriorityQueue`, `ArrayDeque`).
- Complexity intuition feeds **[[System Design]]** and everyday performance work.

## Suggested Study Flow
Master complexity first, then arrays/strings and hashing (the highest-frequency interview topics), then trees and graphs, then DP. Practice patterns continuously — recognition beats memorization.

## Interview Focus
- Analyze time/space complexity on the spot.
- Pick the right data structure for the access pattern.
- Recognize patterns: two pointers, sliding window, BFS/DFS, DP, backtracking.
- Explain trade-offs and edge cases before coding.
