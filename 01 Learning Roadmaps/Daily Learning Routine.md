# My Daily Learning & Life Routine

Every day, I'll focus on learning one concept from a selected subject and follow a structured workflow. I may not be able to complete every step consistently every day, but this will be my ideal process.

## 1. Learn the Concept

- Pick a subject and a specific concept.
- Learn the concept by discussing it with **ChatGPT.**
- Ask follow-up questions until I understand it clearly.
- Watch **YouTube videos, Udemy courses**, and read articles or documentation through Google Search to gain different perspectives.

## 2. Capture Knowledge

- Save useful explanations, examples, diagrams, links, and important conversations in **OneNote** as a temporary knowledge repository.
- Keep only information that is genuinely valuable instead of copying everything.

## 3. Test Understanding

- Explain the concept in my own words.
- **Ask ChatGPT to quiz me** with questions ranging from basic to advanced.
- Identify knowledge gaps and revisit weak areas.

## 4. Organize Knowledge

- Convert the refined understanding into permanent notes in **Obsidian** (Markdown).
- Create **Excalidraw** diagrams *whenever visual representation improves understanding*.
- Link notes to related concepts to build a connected knowledge base.

## 5. Reinforce Learning

- Review **Obsidian notes** (or OneNote if needed).
- Create **Anki flashcards** only for facts, formulas, definitions, or concepts that require long-term memorization.
- Revise Anki cards the following morning.

## 6. Reflect & Plan

- Review what I learned today & journal.
- Note any unanswered questions or areas for improvement.
- Plan the next day's learning objectives and priorities.

# Daily Life Routine

Alongside learning, I'll also prioritize:

- Regular exercise
- Healthy meal preparation and eating
- Meditation or mindfulness
- Office work and professional responsibilities
- Reading important news
- Limited (or no) social media
- Quality time with family and friends
- A small amount of entertainment for relaxation
- Adequate sleep and recovery

---

## Guiding Principles

- Consistency is more important than perfection.
- It's okay if I cannot complete every step every day.
- Even making progress on one concept is a successful day.
- The goal is continuous learning, steady improvement, and sustainable habits rather than trying to do everything perfectly.

### The only thing I'd add

A dedicated **"Apply"** step.

Many learners stop after understanding and organizing. Real mastery comes from using the knowledge:

- Build a small feature after learning a programming concept.
    
- Write one UPSC answer after studying a topic.
    
- Explain the concept aloud without notes.
    
- Relate today's concept to current affairs or work.
    

A good rule is:

> **If you haven't used what you learned, you haven't finished learning it.**

This small addition closes the loop and turns knowledge into lasting skill.

---

The only thing I'd change is **the order** and **the number of decisions** you have to make each day.

The goal shouldn't be **"do everything every day."**  
The goal should be **"move the learning forward every day."**

One problem I noticed is that you're doing **capture, organize, revise, and memorize** all in the same session. That's mentally expensive.

I'd reorganize it into **five stages**.

---

# Stage 0 – Prepare Your Mind & Body

Without this, everything else suffers.

- 😴 Sleep
- 🏃 Exercise
- 🧘 Meditation / Prayer
- 🍎 Good food & hydration
	- 📅 Know today's plan

Think of this as preparing your brain before using it.

---

# Stage 1 – Learn (Input)

Pick **ONE concept**.

```
Subject
    ↓
Concept
```

Use

- ChatGPT
- Books
- Official Docs
- YouTube
- Udemy
- Google (only when needed)

Don't jump between sources immediately.

I'd use

```
ChatGPT

↓

Book / Official Docs

↓

Video (only if needed)

↓

Google
```

Every new source should answer a question the previous one couldn't.

---

# Stage 2 – Understand (Thinking)

This is where learning actually happens.

Ask yourself

- What is it?
- Why?
- How?
- Example?
- Real-world use?
- Limitations?
- What does it connect to?

Then ask ChatGPT

- quiz me
- challenge me
- ask interview questions
- ask UPSC questions
- ask "why"

Don't move on until you can explain it.

---

# Stage 3 – Capture (Temporary)

Only now capture information.

I would simplify:

```
OneNote

↓

Temporary

↓

Delete later
```

Copy

- useful conversations
- screenshots
- links
- snippets

Don't worry about organization.

OneNote becomes your inbox.

---

# Stage 4 – Organize (Permanent)

Now ask

"Will I need this again?"

If NO

Forget it.

If YES

Move it into Obsidian.

Each note should contain only

```
Definition

↓

Why

↓

How

↓

Example

↓

Diagram

↓

Connections

↓

References
```

Use Excalidraw only if the concept is easier visually.

---

# Stage 5 – Remember

Ask

"What must stay in my memory?"

Only those go into Anki.

Examples

- Articles
- Dates
- Definitions
- Formulas
- Commands
- Interview questions
- Frequently forgotten facts

Don't make cards for things you naturally understand.

---

# Stage 6 – Apply

This is the stage people skip.

Software

↓

Write code

UPSC

↓

Write answers

Economy

↓

Analyze news

History

↓

Compare events

Knowledge becomes skill here.

---

# Stage 7 – Reflect

At night

Ask only five questions.

```
What did I learn?

What confused me?

What mistake did I make?

What connects with previous knowledge?

What should I study tomorrow?
```

Done.

---

# Revision

I wouldn't revise OneNote.

Only revise

- Obsidian
- Anki
- Error Log

OneNote should mostly be an inbox. Once something important is transferred to Obsidian, you shouldn't need to revisit the original notes often.

---

# Weekly

Instead of studying,

review your learning system.

```
Review Obsidian

↓

Review Anki

↓

Review mistakes

↓

Improve diagrams

↓

Link notes

↓

Plan next week
```

---

# Monthly

Instead of learning new things,

zoom out.

Ask

- What patterns am I seeing?
- Which subject is weak?
- Which notes are duplicates?
- Which concepts connect?

This builds wisdom instead of just knowledge.

---

# One Change I Strongly Recommend

Instead of trying to be **consistent in every activity**, be consistent in **minimum habits**.

For example:

|Activity|Minimum Version|
|---|---|
|Study|Learn **1 concept**|
|Obsidian|Improve **1 note**|
|Anki|Review **10 cards**|
|Exercise|Walk **15 minutes**|
|Meditation|**5 minutes**|
|Reading|**10 pages**|
|Reflection|**3 minutes**|

On good days you'll do more.

On bad days you won't break the habit.
